# Project Ideas and JavaScript Practice

## Part 1: Five Project Ideas

### 1. Stock Manager for Small Shops

- **Source:** Observing how small shops keep their records, and looking at reviews of stock-management apps.
- **Problem:** Some shop owners may find it hard to know the quantity of goods they have left when they record sales in a notebook.
- **Who has it:** Small shop owners and traders.
- **How they cope today:** They write sales in a notebook or count the goods manually.
- **Possible solution:** I could build a simple app where a shop owner can add products, record sales, and see when an item is almost finished.

### 2. Student Assignment Reminder

- **Source:** Conversations with students and student group discussions.
- **Problem:** Students sometimes forget assignment deadlines because information is kept in different places.
- **Who has it:** Secondary school and university students.
- **How they cope today:** They use notebooks, WhatsApp messages, or phone alarms.
- **Possible solution:** I could build an app where students enter assignments and due dates and mark work as completed.

### 3. Church Event Registration

- **Source:** Observing how church events are organised and how members receive announcements.
- **Problem:** It can take time for an organiser to contact members one by one to find out who will attend an event.
- **Who has it:** Church secretaries and event organisers.
- **How they cope today:** They make phone calls, send WhatsApp messages, or write names on paper.
- **Possible solution:** I could make a page with an event link where people can confirm attendance and the organiser can see the responses.

### 4. Local Service Finder

- **Source:** Community Facebook groups and online posts where people ask for recommendations.
- **Problem:** People may not know how to find a reliable plumber, electrician, tailor, or phone repairer nearby.
- **Who has it:** People who need repairs or other local services.
- **How they cope today:** They ask friends, relatives, neighbours, or people in online groups.
- **Possible solution:** I could build a directory where users search for a type of service and find the provider's contact details and location.

### 5. Keke Fare Guide

- **Source:** Observing transport activities and talking with passengers or drivers.
- **Problem:** A passenger may not know the expected fare for a route before starting the journey.
- **Who has it:** Keke passengers and drivers.
- **How they cope today:** They ask the driver or other passengers and agree on a fare.
- **Possible solution:** I could create a simple route guide with estimated fares. The app would explain that prices can change, so the amounts would need to be checked regularly.

## The Idea I Would Choose

I would choose the **Stock Manager for Small Shops**. I chose it because keeping track of products and sales is important to a shop, and I can start with a few basic features. The other ideas are useful too, but some of them would need more information from different people or more work to keep the information accurate.

## The Smallest Version I Could Build in Two Weeks

The first version would allow a user to:

1. Add a product name, price, and quantity.
2. View the products in a list.
3. Record a sale.
4. Reduce the stock quantity after a sale.
5. See a warning when a product is running low.

I would use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript for the first version. I would not add login, online payments, barcode scanning, or complicated reports yet, because I want to finish the main features first.

### Two-week plan

- **Days 1–2:** Ask a few shop owners how they currently record sales and stock.
- **Days 3–4:** Create the page layout with HTML and CSS.
- **Days 5–8:** Write the JavaScript for adding products and recording sales.
- **Days 9–10:** Add low-stock warnings and check that the inputs work properly.
- **Days 11–12:** Test the app and fix errors.
- **Days 13–14:** Show it to a shop owner and make changes based on their feedback.

## Who I Could Show It To

I could show the app to a shop owner I know, especially someone who writes stock records in a notebook. I would ask whether the app is easy to use and what they would change. Before writing that a person has agreed to test it, I would ask them first.
